# Artificial Intelligence on Instagram: Creativity or a Crisis of Trust?

**Activity:** Collaborative Discussion – Initial Post  
**Date posted:** 4 August 2026

When I first started using Instagram, most posts appeared to represent people’s daily lives and genuine experiences. Today, however, it is increasingly difficult to determine whether an image, video or even a person shown on the platform is real. One major reason for this change is the growing use of artificial intelligence (AI) within the social media industry.

Instagram uses AI-powered recommender systems to analyse user behaviour and decide which posts appear on the Explore page and Reels feed. This algorithmic personalisation helps users discover relevant content while allowing businesses, particularly smaller companies, to reach specific audiences more efficiently. Saheb, Sidaoui and Schmarzo (2024) argue that the convergence of AI and social media creates opportunities for personalisation, marketing and user engagement.

Nevertheless, personalisation does not always benefit users. Algorithms designed to maximise engagement may repeatedly present similar content instead of exposing individuals to diverse perspectives. Lorenz-Spreen et al. (2023) found that digital media can improve access to information and encourage participation, but it may also contribute to political polarisation and declining institutional trust. The same technology can therefore produce commercial benefits and negative societal consequences.

Generative AI has made this challenge more complex because realistic but false images and videos can now be produced quickly and at a low cost. Misinformation may consequently become more persuasive and difficult to identify (Raman et al., 2024). As a recent example, Meta announced in 2024 that it would label AI-generated images posted on Instagram, Facebook and Threads (Meta, 2024). Although this represents a step towards transparency, its effectiveness is limited because not every AI-generated item contains detectable technical markers.

In my view, AI has made Instagram more creative, personalised and commercially accessible. However, its benefits should be supported by transparent labelling, accountable algorithms, human oversight and improved digital literacy to protect user trust.
